One of my favorite projects to date has been leading the user research and
design for an [internal survey management tool](/projects/survey-management-tool)
at a market research firm. The teams there were working across several
different systems, which put a dent in their productivity and made it hard to
execute projects well.

What made this one special was how much of the process happened away from the
screen. I started by shadowing teams from each department to learn how they
actually serviced customers and fulfilled orders, which is almost never how
the documentation says they do. I interviewed team members about their pain
points, built personas, and mapped out a workflow diagram showing how often
people were bouncing between applications and each other.

Only after all of that did the design work start. I aligned with stakeholders
on goals, worked side by side with the Operations team to imagine what a new
application could do, and we ranked every need to settle on an MVP. Wireframes
came next, then a functioning prototype, then several rounds of feedback
before the UX settled.

The lesson I keep taking away from projects like this: the quality of the
solution is set long before anyone opens a design tool. It's set by how well
you understand the problem.
